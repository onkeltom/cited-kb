# Later

English is the source for this page. The README links here from every language.

The knowledge base is for your own learning. Build it until a question is solid: cards, categories you can explain, accepted tags, a packet you can reread. The uses below come after that. Each one starts from `cited.py brief`, not from the transcript folder.

## A note to yourself

Once a month, open the brief for one question and write what you now think. Date it. Label the sentences that are yours. The brief stays the evidence. The note is the learning.

## A memo, a post, a talk

Use the same brief. Check any quote with `cited.py show <id> --at mm:ss --window 1`. Keep three layers visible: the categories you froze, the wider set of episodes, and your conclusion. A post can point people at the episodes you learned from. It should not read as if those episodes proved your conclusion.

## A handoff

Send someone the brief instead of a playlist. They get the claims, the timestamps, and the flags for a guest who sells the tool or an episode that was sponsored. They can disagree with your categories without relistening to the hours.

## A dated research file

One question, one file, refreshed when you accept more tags. Put the codebook date on it. That stops "I learned this" from sliding into "this has always been true."

## Teaching the question

The packet is a short syllabus: the definitions, the accepted claims, the gaps on the cards, and the few episodes you would actually assign. Assign those. Do not assign the folder.

## A shared codebook

More than one person can walk the same categories. Do that after you can apply them alone. A group that starts from the placeholder categories inherits a theory nobody can explain.
